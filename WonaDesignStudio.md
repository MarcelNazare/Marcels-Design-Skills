# design.md — Wona Design Studio

> **Crafting Brands, Inspiring Connections.**

This file defines the design philosophy, principles, and system that govern every website project Wona Design Studio delivers. It is not a template — it is a way of thinking. Every project starts here and ends with a brand that feels authentic, strategic, and unmistakably its own.

---

## 1. The Feeling We Deliver

Every Wona project should leave the visitor with a distinct emotional impression. Design is not decoration — it is the feeling a brand leaves behind. We design for these feelings:

### **Confidence**
The visitor should feel that this brand knows exactly who it is. Clarity of layout, decisive typography, and intentional whitespace create the sense that nothing is accidental. Strategy made visible.

### **Warmth**
Nothing should feel cold or corporate. Rounded edges, generous space, natural tones, and human language invite the visitor in. The brand feels approachable — like a conversation, not a pitch.

### **Craft**
Every detail should feel considered. Alignment, rhythm, contrast, and micro-interactions signal that this brand cares about quality. Craft is the proof of professionalism.

### **Momentum**
The brand should feel alive and moving forward — not static. Subtle motion, clear pathways, and strong calls to action create a sense of possibility. The visitor should feel invited to take the next step.

### **Authenticity**
No templates. No borrowed aesthetics. Every project should feel like it could only belong to this brand. The design should reveal the vision behind the business, not mask it.

**The Wona feeling in one line:** *"This brand understands me, and I trust where it's going."*

## 2. Design Principles

These principles apply to every website project, regardless of industry or scope.

### 2.1 Strategy Before Aesthetics
Every design decision must trace back to a strategic reason. Before choosing a color, a typeface, or a layout, we ask: *What is this brand positioning, and does this decision support it?* Beauty without purpose is decoration. We build brands that look exceptional **and** perform measurably.

### 2.2 Clarity Is Kindness
We speak in a warm, clear, and encouraging voice — and the design must reflect that. Clear hierarchy, readable type, obvious navigation, and honest messaging. The visitor should never feel lost or overwhelmed. Confusion is a design failure.

### 2.3 Personality Over Polish
A brand should be as unique as the vision behind it. We favor distinctive, characterful design over safe, generic polish. Every project should carry a recognizable point of view — in its typography, its color, its rhythm, its voice.

### 2.4 Collaboration Made Visible
Our process is personal and collaborative. The website should feel like it was made *with* the client, not *for* them. This shows up as authenticity in copy, specificity in imagery, and a sense of ownership in the details.

### 2.5 Measurable Outcomes
Design is accountable. Every project defines success criteria up front — clarity of message, conversion paths, engagement, or brand recall. Design decisions should be defensible against those outcomes.

## 3. Visual Language

### 3.1 Color
- **Base:** Off-white, cream, or soft neutral backgrounds. Charcoal or deep warm black for text.
- **Accent:** One primary accent per project — muted and sophisticated (terracotta, sage, warm ochre, dusty blue, etc.). Accents should feel chosen, not default.
- **Rules:**
  - Maximum of one accent color per brand system unless the brand strategy explicitly calls for more.
  - Avoid pure black (#000) and pure white (#FFF) — always soften.
  - Color must carry meaning, not just fill space.

### 3.2 Typography
- **Headlines:** Expressive serif or high-quality display face. Personality lives here.
- **Body:** Clean, highly legible sans-serif. Warmth and clarity live here.
- **Rules:**
  - Establish a clear typographic scale (e.g., 1.25 or 1.333 ratio).
  - Limit to two typefaces per project (display + body) unless there is a strategic reason for more.
  - Generous line-height for body copy (1.5–1.7).
  - Avoid trendy fonts that will date the brand within two years.

### 3.3 Layout & Space
- Generous whitespace is a core signature of Wona work. Space signals confidence and craft.
- Use a disciplined grid, then break it intentionally — never accidentally.
- Asymmetry and editorial composition are welcome when they serve hierarchy.
- Mobile-first thinking: the experience must feel complete on the smallest screen.

### 3.4 Imagery
- Prefer authentic, brand-specific imagery: mockups, typography details, packaging, editorial layouts, studio moments, real people.
- Avoid generic stock photography and clichéd "business" imagery.
- Imagery should feel like it belongs to *this* brand — not any brand.

### 3.5 Motion
- Motion should be subtle, purposeful, and warm — never flashy or distracting.
- Use fade-ins, gentle reveals, hover states, and smooth transitions.
- Motion should guide attention and create momentum, not perform.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` in every project.

## 4. Voice & Copy Direction

The website's words are part of the design. All copy should feel like it was written by a person, not generated.

- **Tone:** Warm, clear, encouraging, confident.
- **Personality:** Creative, visionary, approachable, professional.
- **Avoid:** Corporate jargon, hype language, generic filler, exclamation overload.
- **Favor:** Specific, human, direct language that invites collaboration.
- **Every page should answer:** *Who is this for, what do they get, and why does it matter?*

---

## 5. Page-Level Design Standards

Every page in every project must satisfy these standards:

| Standard | Requirement |
|----------|-------------|
| **Hero clarity** | Within 5 seconds, a visitor understands who the brand is for and what it offers. |
| **One primary CTA** | Each page has a single, obvious next step. Secondary CTAs are clearly subordinate. |
| **Scannable hierarchy** | Headlines, subheads, and body text form a clear visual ladder. |
| **Human imagery** | At least one authentic, brand-specific visual per major section. |
| **Warmth in microcopy** | Buttons, forms, and empty states use encouraging, human language. |
| **Mobile completeness** | Every section is fully usable and beautiful on mobile. |
| **Performance** | Fast load, optimized images, accessible contrast and semantics. |

## 6. Signature Components

These recurring components define the Wona feel across projects:

- **The Positioning Statement** — A short, confident paragraph near the top of the homepage that states who the brand serves and what it delivers.
- **The Filterable Work Grid** — A portfolio section with category filters, project cards, and clear case-study pathways.
- **The Demo Experience** — An interactive sample section (used where relevant) that lets visitors explore a brand system hands-on.
- **The Process Ladder** — A 4-step collaborative process (Discover → Define → Design → Deliver) presented with warmth and clarity.
- **The Human CTA** — A final call to action written like an invitation, not a demand: *"Let's build a brand as unique as your vision."*

---

## 7. Accessibility & Inclusivity

Wona brands are for ambitious people of all backgrounds. Design must be inclusive by default:

- Minimum WCAG AA contrast for all text.
- Keyboard-navigable interactions.
- Semantic HTML and meaningful alt text.
- Motion respects user preferences.
- Language is clear, welcoming, and free of exclusionary jargon.

---

## 8. What We Avoid

To protect the Wona feeling, every project must avoid:

- Template aesthetics and generic layouts.
- Cold, corporate, or overly technical visual language.
- Trend-chasing that will date the brand.
- Clutter, visual noise, and unnecessary decoration.
- Copy that sounds like it was written by a machine.
- Motion or interaction that distracts from clarity.

---

## 9. The Wona Design Test

Before any project is delivered, it must pass these five questions:

1. **Does it feel warm?** Would a visitor feel invited in?
2. **Does it feel confident?** Does the brand feel like it knows who it is?
3. **Does it feel crafted?** Are the details considered and consistent?
4. **Does it feel unique?** Could this only belong to this brand?
5. **Does it feel strategic?** Can every major decision be traced to a clear purpose?

If any answer is no, the work is not finished.
